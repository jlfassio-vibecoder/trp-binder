FORMAL LEGAL SUBMISSION BRIEF

In the Matter of an Application for a Temporary Resident Permit (TRP) pursuant to Section 24(1) of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (IRPA)

TO: Designated Decision-Maker / Senior Immigration Officer
APPLICANT: Justin Louis Hardy FASSIO
UCI: 11-2962-5822

I EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & STATUTORY BASIS

1. This application respectfully requests the issuance of a Temporary Resident Permit (TRP) pursuant to Subsection 24(1) of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (IRPA). The Applicant, Justin Louis Hardy FASSIO, is a citizen of the United States of America and the equal joint-guardian of a six-year-old Canadian Citizen child, Ava FASSIO, who resides in Kelowna, British Columbia.

2. The issuance of a TRP is justified by compelling humanitarian factors, specifically the Best Interests of the Child (BIOC) as articulated by the Supreme Court of Canada in Kanthasamy v. Canada (Citizenship and Immigration), 2015 SCC 61, weighed against a technical non-compliance finding recorded under IRPA ss. 41(a) and 20(1)(a) at the Osoyoos Port of Entry on June 3, 2026.

Statutory Balancing Framework (IRPA s. 24(1)):

An officer evaluating a TRP request must balance the compelling need of the applicant to enter or remain in Canada against any risk to Canadian society or program integrity (CBSA Operational Manual ENF 23). The Applicant presents zero criminality, an unbroken history of voluntary compliance, and substantial financial self-sufficiency.

II THE LEGISLATIVE VOID & THE CUSTODY PARADOX

3. The Relocation Prohibition: Under a binding Order of the Supreme Court of British Columbia (Kelowna Registry, File No. 139323, Term 16), Ava FASSIO is legally prohibited from being relocated outside the province of British Columbia. The Applicant and the child's mother share 50/50 joint physical custody and guardianship.

4. The Legislative Void: Canadian immigration legislation contains no dedicated temporary or permanent visa stream for foreign national parents of minor Canadian citizen children who are bound by Canadian family court mandates.

5. The Custody Paradox: Refusing entry to the father creates a structural paradox. The Supreme Court of British Columbia mandates that the child remain in Kelowna with equal access to both parents, while the immigration authority excludes the father from the country. This paradox directly violates BIOC principles by stripping a 6-year-old Canadian child of her court-ordered equal parent.

III REBUTTAL OF CBSA GCMS FINDINGS (FILE P-2026-30692)

1. Uncodified Legal Threshold:

The inspecting officer at Osoyoos POE explicitly stated in GCMS notes that an applicant "must spend the majority of his time outside of Canada." This requirement appears nowhere in the IRPA or IRPR. Applying an uncodified, informal "rule of thumb" constitutes an error of law and a fettering of discretion (Stemijon Investments Ltd. v. Canada, 2011 FCA 299).

2. Statutory Dual Intent (IRPA s. 22(2)):

Holding a desire to secure permanent residence in the future does not preclude temporary entry (Jewell v. Canada, 2015 FC 1046; Williams v. Canada, 2020 FC 8). The Applicant's prior 2021 Express Entry profile (E002017531) demonstrates transparent, lawful aspirations, not non-compliant intent.

3. Procedural Defect in s. 41(a) Charge:

The primary GCMS charge under IRPA s. 41(a) contains a blank "specifically:" clause. Leaving the core operational field blank undercuts administrative notice and procedural fairness under Vavilov.

4. Misconstrued U.S. Domicile & Fiduciary Ties:

The officer erroneously noted "few U.S. ties" while ignoring the Applicant's active Power of Attorney and primary beneficiary status under a California Revocable Living Trust holding $2.3M in Aptos real estate, alongside VA disability pension ties.

5. Unbroken 100% Compliance History:

The Applicant departed Canada immediately upon his September 23, 2024 VR extension refusal (due to strike-related passport delays) and voluntarily signed an Allowed to Leave (IMM 1282) on June 3, 2026. He has zero history of overstaying.

IV CONCLUSION & RELIEF SOUGHT

6. The Applicant meets all statutory criteria for a Temporary Resident Permit under IRPA s. 24(1). The compelling need to parent a minor Canadian child under a Supreme Court mandate far outweighs any administrative program-integrity risk.

RELIEF REQUESTED: That a Temporary Resident Permit (TRP) be issued for a duration of 12 to 24 months, with conditions authorizing visitor status to fulfill court-ordered parenting obligations in Kelowna, British Columbia.
