## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Recommended path to a 1–3 month visit, lowest risk first:**

**Mail the complete outland TRP package to the LA Consulate immediately (this week), then attempt ONE carefully documented, bounded visitor entry at a full-service land POE after a defined waiting window (target: 4–6 weeks post-mailing), carrying a complete duplicate TRP package as a POE fallback of last resort — with counsel sign-off from Ocana Law before the crossing.**

Three findings from this research cycle change the risk calculus and must drive sequencing:

1. **The repeat-encounter downside is now specifically quantified, and it is worse than previously modeled.** Under IRPR s.228(1)(c)(iii), if a second officer makes the *same* finding (failure to establish temporary intent under s.20), the s.44 report is **not** referred to the Immigration Division — the Minister's Delegate at the POE issues an **exclusion order on the spot**. An exclusion order = a 12-month bar on re-entry (ARC required to return sooner). IRPR s.228(1.1) creates the same exclusion-order channel where an officer directs a temporary return to the U.S. under IRPR s.41 and the person fails to establish departure intent. June 3 ended in an ATL — the most lenient available outcome. **A failed second attempt is unlikely to end as gently.** This is the single most important number in this brief: the cost of a failed Stage 1 entry is not "another ATL" — it is realistically **12 months of zero parenting time**, which would itself devastate the Term 12 / 50-50 parenting structure.
2. **The TRP fee changed December 1, 2025: it is now CAD $246.25** (previously $239.75). The prior brief and any drafted cover materials citing $239.75 must be corrected before mailing. IRCC has signaled annual fee adjustments going forward — re-verify on the official fee list the week of mailing.
3. **A mailed-but-unacknowledged consular package has no GCMS footprint.** Until the LA Consulate receipts the package and opens a file, there is nothing in GCMS for a border officer to "defer to." Proof of mailing is therefore **good-faith evidence, not a pending application an officer can coordinate with.** This reframes strategy element 3 (the at-POE pivot): it is real and lawful, but it is a fresh discretionary ask, not a hand-off.

**Bottom line:** The outland TRP is the only track with **zero** enforcement downside, so it goes first in time even though it has no timeline. The visitor entry is the only track with **speed**, so it carries the visit — but exactly once, maximally documented, and only after counsel review, because the downside of failure is now a probable 12-month bar rather than a repeat ATL.

## 5. STAGE 1 DEEP-DIVE — WHAT THE RESEARCH ACTUALLY SUPPORTS

### 5.1 POE selection: verified answer
There is **no official evidence** that larger POEs adjudicate visitor admissibility more favorably. The June 3 GCMS narrative is equally visible everywhere; the merits travel with the file, not the building. The **only** verified operational advantages of a large POE (Pacific Highway/Douglas):
- Designated TRP decision-makers and supervisors are more likely to be on shift (relevant *only* if the fallback branch is ever reached — CBSA's own audit notes officers at smaller/less-experienced postings flag needing more support on TRP thresholds);
- Full-service immigration secondary with longer hours.

Avoiding Osoyoos specifically has one additional, human rationale: not re-encountering the same officer or immediate colleagues while the June 3 file is fresh. That is legitimate encounter-management, not forum shopping — say nothing about POE choice if asked; the itinerary (a coastal-route camping trip) should itself make Pacific Highway the natural crossing. **The itinerary should genuinely fit the crossing.**

### 5.2 The at-POE TRP pivot (strategy element 3): verified answer
- **Coherent? Yes, mechanically.** ENF 4 §15.3 defines initiating a TRP at the POE; designated CBSA officers can and do issue them; decision same-day.
- **Will the officer defer to the consular filing? No.** Until the LA package is receipted into GCMS, it does not exist to the officer. Even once receipted, there is **no coordination or deference mechanism** — the POE TRP is an independent discretionary decision. Proof of tracked mailing helps only as *good-faith/channel-compliance evidence* ("I am using the correct process; this entry request is not an attempt to shortcut it"). It cannot hurt, but it converts nothing.
- **The real cost:** requesting a TRP at the counter is a formal concession of inadmissibility in that encounter. If the officer refuses the TRP *and* writes the s.44 report on the same 20(1)(a)/41(a) basis, R228(1)(c)(iii) channels the outcome to an **exclusion order without a hearing**. That is why the fallback sits behind (a) supervisor request and (b) the counsel-scripted withdraw-vs-request decision point — and why it is a prepared contingency, never the plan.

### 5.3 Repeated bounded entries and the cumulative-pattern problem (strategy element 4)
- **Legally sound? Yes.** IRPR s.183 evaluates each entry on its own; no statute or regulation caps cumulative visitor presence; s.22(2) plus the April 2023 dual-intent instructions make the two-intent posture expressly legitimate. The officer's "majority of time outside Canada" standard remains an invented test (the *Stemijon* fettering argument in the project file stands).
- **But the June 3 narrative shows exactly how a pattern gets weaponized** ("reset his clock"). Mitigations, in the documents rather than in argument at the counter:
  1. Every entry has a **dated end before it begins** — reservations and U.S.-side commitments that exist *prior* to the crossing;
  2. **Meaningful U.S. intervals** between visits with a documented U.S. life (mail volume, medical, family-property duties under the POA, vehicle registration/insurance at the Santa Cruz address) — not 24-hour turnarounds like the Oct 23→24, 2025 pair, which is the single worst optic in the travel table;
  3. The **vehicle story stays forward-looking**: the RAM's failure explains the gap; the Crosstrek's economics explain why the bounded-visit cadence is now durable. Invoices and purchase documents in the binder;
  4. Keep the Kelowna lease framed in writing as what it is — the operational cost of complying with a BC court order that locks Ava to BC — and pair it with the U.S.-domicile evidence, so "renting a condo 18 months" reads as parenting logistics, not settlement. (ENF 4 itself cautions officers against single-factor denial such as renting local property.)
